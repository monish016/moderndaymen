Who it's for

Two readers. One book.

For the man at 2:47

You don't have to finish it. Open any chapter that sounds like your address.

No mantra. What happened to me and to men like us, how we came back, and at the end of each chapter one small thing that helped. Take it or leave it. Short chapters, readable on a phone, in any order, on the nights when nothing else is readable.

For the one who loves him

Worried about a man in your life and don't know what to say? Hand him this.

He may never buy a book about being a man. He will read the one you put in his hand. The prologue does the talking you can't, the helplines are printed where he'll find them, and there is a page at the back written for you, on what to say and where your job ends.

Prologue: 2:47 AM

The whole prologue arrives as a PDF. When pre-orders open, you hear first.

2:47 AM. A rented house, a thousand kilometres from the town I still call home.

The fridge hums. That is the whole sound of the house.

I am at the dining table with a laptop, a headset, and tea that went cold around midnight. My client is on the other side of the planet. For him it is afternoon. A message pops up. Great work today. You're a lifesaver. I look at that word for a while. Somewhere across the world, a man I have never met thinks I have my life together.

Nobody tells you about this hour. The work ends. The screen goes quiet. And there is nothing left between you and your own head. The questions come in order. They always come in order.

Is it like this for everyone? Am I the bad guy here? Shouldn't men be happy?

At 3:10 AM, I opened a browser. Not for the client. For myself. I started typing a question I was too ashamed to ask any living person, and the search box finished my sentence before I could.

That is the first page.
